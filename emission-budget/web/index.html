Spending the 21st Century Emission Budget

Warming grows in step with all the carbon we've ever emitted. So every year's emissions add a little warming that stays with us. Choose how fast the world cuts its emissions, starting now, and see how warm the rest of the century gets.

Open in a new window

The world's carbon emissions from fossil fuels and land use, cut by the same share every year from 2026. In 2025 they were about 11.5 GtC.

The budget. Chapter 9 of the book says warming grows by about 2 °C for every 1,000 GtC emitted. People have emitted about 757 GtC so far. To stay below 2 °C, the world can emit only about 244 GtC more, ever.

This is a very simple model. It has one slider, one rule for the emissions and one for the warming. The About tab has every equation.

About this model

This is a very simple model of the world's carbon emissions and the warming they cause, from now to 2100. It rests on one idea from Chapter 9, "The One-Way Thermostat": the warming grows in proportion to all the carbon people have ever emitted. That ratio is the transient climate response to cumulative emissions, or TCRE. The book's teaching value is 2 °C for every 1,000 GtC (a GtC is a billion tonnes of carbon).

So the warming works like an emission budget. By the end of 2025, people had emitted about 756.5 GtC, enough for 1.5 °C. Staying below 2 °C means emitting no more than 1,000 GtC in all, so only about 243.5 GtC more, ever. In 2025 the world emitted about 11.5 GtC. At that rate, the warming passes 2 °C in 2048.

On this page, you choose how fast the world cuts its emissions, by the same share every year. On the page as it opens, they're cut 2% a year. Then the emissions fall to 2.6 GtC a year by 2100, the warming passes 2 °C in 2054, and it reaches 2.4 °C (4.3 °F) in 2100. Cut faster, and the warming stays lower. Cut 4.8% a year, and it never passes 2 °C.

How to use this page

The page has two graphs. The top one shows the world's carbon emissions from fossil fuels and land use each year, and the bottom one shows the warming since the late 1800s. Each graph has two lines. The gray line keeps the emissions at 11.5 GtC every year, and the green line uses your cuts. The dashed red line marks 2 °C of warming above preindustrial conditions.

You can change one thing: how much the emissions are cut each year, from 0% to 10%. The page opens at 2%.

Move over a graph to read the numbers for one year. Or select the graph and press the left or right arrow key. The first press starts in 2050, and each press after it moves a year, or ten years with Shift. Home goes to 2026 and End to 2100. Below the graph, a few lines sum up 2100 and say where the warming would end. "Back to where the page opened" puts the slider back at 2%.

The address in your browser changes as you move the slider, so you can bookmark a view or send it to someone. Nothing is sent anywhere as you work.

This page is part of the website for Climate Change: Simple, Serious, Solvable, an open textbook by Scott Denning. It goes with Chapter 9, "The One-Way Thermostat".

The equations

You don't need any of this to use the page. But the whole model is four short equations, and you can follow every one of them with a calculator.

The model answers one question: if the world cuts its emissions by the same share every year, how warm does it get?

You choose one thing: r, how much the emissions are cut, in % a year. The rest is fixed:

WhatIts value
The model's first year2026
The emissions in the first year, E0, from fossil fuels and land use: 2025's11.5 GtC a year
All the carbon emitted from 1850 through 2025, S0756.5 GtC
The warming for each GtC emitted (TCRE)2 °C for every 1,000 GtC

1. How much does the world emit?

Each year the emissions are cut by r percent of what they were the year before. So n years after 2026, the world emits

E=E0⁢(1−r100)n

GtC a year. Each year's cut is a share of a smaller number, so the cuts get smaller in GtC as the emissions shrink, and the emissions never quite reach zero. It's compound interest running backward. At 2% a year, the emissions halve about every 34 years.

2. How much carbon has been emitted in all?

Start with the 756.5 GtC emitted before 2026, and add each year's emissions. By the start of year n, the total is

S=S0+100⁢E0⁢(1−(1−r100)n)r

GtC. The model adds up the years one at a time. This equation is the shortcut: the sum of numbers that each shrink by the same share, which a calculator can do in one go.

3. How much warmer does it get?

This is TCRE, from Chapter 9: the warming grows in proportion to all the carbon emitted, at 2 °C for every 1,000 GtC. So the warming is

T=21,000⁢S

degrees Celsius above the late 1800s. When the emissions stop, S stops growing, and so does the warming. Each GtC adds 0.002 °C that stays with us, so 11.5 GtC a year adds 0.023 °C every year.

4. Where does it end?

The emissions never reach zero, but they shrink so fast that their total doesn't grow forever. If the cuts go on after 2100, all the carbon there will ever be is

S∞=S0+100⁢E0r

GtC. That's step 2 with the years going on forever: the part in brackets shrinks to nothing. So the carbon still to come, from 2026 on, is E0 times 100/r. At 2% a year, that's 50 years of today's emissions. Step 3 turns S∞ into the warming where the line on the graph is heading.

Turn it around, and it says how fast the cuts must be to stay below 2 °C. The budget of 243.5 GtC is 100 × 11.5 ÷ r, so r = 1,150 ÷ 243.5 = 4.72% a year. The slider moves in steps of 0.1, so on the slider that's 4.8%.

Where the equations stop

  • No cuts. At r = 0, steps 2 and 4 divide by zero. With no cuts, the world emits 11.5 GtC every year, so the total is just S = S0 + 11.5 n, and the warming never stops rising. That's the gray line.
  • Only carbon dioxide. TCRE is the warming from CO₂. Methane, the other gases and the smog that cools (Chapter 4) are left out. The model starts at 1.5 °C, a little above the 1.4 °C the world had warmed by 2025 (Chapter 1).
  • A round number for TCRE. The IPCC's best estimate is about 1.65 °C for every 1,000 GtC, and it's likely between about 1.0 and 2.3. The book's 2 °C makes the arithmetic easy and lies inside that range.
  • The same share every year. Real cuts could be faster or slower from year to year, and could reach zero. Only the total matters for the warming, so any path that emits the same carbon by 2100 ends at the same warming in 2100.
  • No removal. Nothing takes carbon back out of the air on purpose. The total only grows, so the warming never comes down.

A worked example

Open the page and leave the slider at 2%. Select the graph and press the right arrow key once, to 2050. Here's where the page's numbers come from.

  1. The emissions in 2050 (step 1). 2050 is 24 years after 2026, so n = 24. 1 − 2/100 = 0.98, and 0.9824 = 0.6158. E = 11.5 × 0.6158 = 7.08 GtC a year.
  2. All the carbon emitted by 2050 (step 2). 1 − 0.6158 = 0.3842. S = 756.5 + 100 × 11.5 × 0.3842 ÷ 2 = 756.5 + 220.9 = 977.4 GtC.
  3. The warming in 2050 (step 3). T = 0.002 × 977.4 = 1.95 °C.

The page says that in 2050, with your cuts, the world emits 7.08 GtC, 977 GtC has been emitted in all and the warming is 1.95 °C. And step 4 says where it ends: S∞ = 756.5 + 100 × 11.5 ÷ 2 = 1,331.5 GtC, and 0.002 × 1,331.5 = 2.66 °C.

A few details

  • The start of each year. Each year's total and warming count the carbon emitted before that year began. So in 2026 the total is 756.5 GtC and the warming 1.5 °C, and the warming in 2100 doesn't yet count the carbon emitted during 2100.
  • Fossil fuels and land use together. The 11.5 GtC is about 10.4 from burning fossil fuels and about 1.1 from clearing land, after counting the forests that grow back. The model cuts both by the same share.
  • Live numbers. Each January, the book brings its fast-changing numbers up to date. This page moves with them: its first year, E0, S0 and every number on this tab that depends on them.

See the equations at work

  1. No cuts. Set the slider to 0. The world emits 11.5 GtC every year, the warming passes 2 °C in 2048, and it reaches 3.2 °C by 2100, still rising 0.023 °C a year (steps 2 and 3).
  2. Slow cuts. Set the slider to 1. The emissions in 2100 are still 5.5 GtC a year, and the warming passes 2 °C in 2050, only 2 years later than with no cuts. If the cuts go on, the warming ends at 3.8 °C (step 4).
  3. Just enough. Set the slider to 4.8. The warming in 2100 is 1.98 °C, and if the cuts go on, it ends at 1.99 °C, just under the dashed line (step 4).
  4. Fast cuts. Set the slider to 10. The emissions in 2050 are 0.92 GtC a year, and the warming ends at 1.74 °C. Even the fastest cuts on the page add 115 GtC, and 0.23 °C, to the warming we already have.

Where the numbers come from

The emissions in 2025, about 10.4 GtC from fossil fuels and 1.1 from land use, are the Global Carbon Budget's. So is the 745.0 GtC emitted from 1850 through 2024, counting fossil fuels and land use. Add 2025's 11.5 GtC, and S0 is 756.5 GtC. Chapter 9 rounds the total through 2024 to about 750 GtC.

Friedlingstein, P., et al. (2026). Global Carbon Budget 2025. Earth System Science Data, 18, 3211–3288. doi:10.5194/essd-18-3211-2026

The 2 °C for every 1,000 GtC is the book's teaching value for TCRE, a round number inside the range the IPCC assessed:

IPCC (2021). Summary for Policymakers. In Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis. Contribution of Working Group I to the Sixth Assessment Report of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change. Cambridge University Press. doi:10.1017/9781009157896.001

How it was checked

A set of tests comes with the model, and they run every time the page changes. Here's what they hold it to.

  • The code, against a second copy. The tests have the equations written out a second time, in another programming language. At every setting they try, the two copies agree to within a billionth.
  • The shortcuts, against the long way. The equations of steps 2 and 4 are tried against the years added up one at a time, out to thousands of years for step 4.
  • The model, against the book. Its starting numbers are the book's live numbers, and its 2 °C for every 1,000 GtC is the book's TCRE.
  • This tab, against the model. Every equation above is tried with the model's own numbers. The worked example is done again from the rounded numbers a calculator would use. And each number in the text is worked out again from the model.

The code

The code is small enough to read. Two files do all the work, and they use no outside software. The comments in model.js use the same step numbers and letters as the equations above.

model.js is the science: the four steps, and the loop that runs them to 2100.


app.js is the page: the slider, the graphs and the lines under them.


To keep a copy, save model.js or app.js.
